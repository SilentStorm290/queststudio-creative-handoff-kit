Product Creative Review Field Guide

A polished image can still show the wrong product. Separate the things you can change in a campaign from the details that must remain faithful to the approved reference.

Published by the QuestStudio team · November 14, 2026

Lock the referenceReview identityRecord a decisionTools and examples

1. Lock the reference before making variations

Choose one approved product photograph and keep it beside the working files. Write down the details that identify the item:

  • Silhouette, proportions and construction.
  • Material, texture and stitching.
  • Closures, handles, buttons and other functional parts.
  • Label wording, logos and placement.
  • Color and recognizable surface details.

Record the intended edit separately: a new background, a different crop, a campaign layout or a change in lighting. This gives the reviewer a concrete brief instead of a general request to make the result look better.

2. Check identity before composition

Compare each candidate with the reference at a useful viewing size. Start with the product itself, then review the scene and layout.

Five checks against the approved product photograph
CheckAccept whenReject or hold when
ShapeThe visible silhouette and proportions match the approved reference.The item gains or loses parts, its construction changes, or another view is needed to resolve a difference.
MaterialSurface details still represent the original material.Fabric becomes a different texture or finish.
LabelsRequired wording and marks remain correct and readable.Text is invented, misspelled or moved to a misleading position.
Functional detailsClosures, handles and fittings match the approved source and included-parts record.Hardware is duplicated, missing, impossible or unsupported by the source.
LayoutThe crop preserves the details needed to understand the item.A crop conceals an important product change.

A beautiful background does not compensate for an incorrect label or changed construction. Keep a rejected image out of the delivery folder even when its composition is attractive.

3. Use explicit review states

Give every candidate one status: hold, revise and recheck, approved for a named use, or rejected. Add a short reason tied to the checklist, such as “zipper construction changed” or “product details match, crop needs more room.” Hold a decision when a detail is hidden or the source cannot establish it, and name the person who will provide the missing evidence.

For a revision, record the specific detail to preserve and the specific scene element to change. Avoid using an already altered product as the next reference unless that change has been intentionally approved.

4. Build the campaign from approved assets

Once the product passes review, plan the campaign variants. A useful starting set is a hero image, a tighter detail crop and a vertical ad layout. Check each version again: a crop or layout change can hide a label, cut through a handle or create a misleading impression of scale.

For a short video, decide what each shot must communicate before generating additional clips. Keep the product-reference checks in the shot brief so consistency remains part of the handoff.

5. Hand off a small, clear packet

Include the approved reference, the final selected files, required aspect ratios, captions or on-screen copy, and a note identifying the approved versions. Keep revision notes with the working files. Label illustrative examples as examples so they are not mistaken for a real client result.
